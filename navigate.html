Chapter 2

Navigating Graphs

Finding your way through a graph is like finding your way through a maze. Build a route and find out whether it is a walk, a trail, a path, a closed walk, a circuit or a directed cycle.

Tool

Your route

Click vertices in order, or type the names with spaces, like b c d.

Open routes

Walksany route along the edges
Trailsno repeated edge
Pathsno repeated edge or vertex

Closed routes (start and end at the same vertex)

Closed walksany closed route along the edges
Circuits (closed trails)no repeated edge
Directed cycles (closed paths)no repeated edge or vertex

Words to know

Walk: a sequence of adjacent vertices and the edges between them. You name it by listing the vertices in order, and a vertex visited twice is listed twice. Every step has to follow an edge, so you cannot jump over a wall.

Trail: a walk that never travels the same edge twice. Path: a walk that never visits the same vertex twice. Every path is a trail, and every trail is a walk.

Closed: the route begins and ends at the same vertex. Beginning and ending at the same vertex does not count as visiting it twice. A closed walk can repeat anything. A circuit is a closed trail, and a directed cycle is a closed path. Every directed cycle is a circuit, and every circuit is a closed walk.

A circuit can be named from any of its vertices: d → f → b → c → d is the same circuit as f → b → c → d → f.
